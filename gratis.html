Die Bladsye

Three pages. One calmer week. Free.

A month, a week and a day — printed, so the whole household can see it. Print as many as you need, stick them on the fridge, write in pencil.

There is one column on every page that matters more than the rest: wie vat dit? Not what's happening — who is actually taking it. Because a thing with nobody's name on it is still yours by default, and that is how the load quietly stays with one person.

What this doesn't do

These pages are honest about their limits. You still type every date in yourself. Nothing reminds anybody. Next term starts as a blank page again.

That last part is the thing we're building: upload the school programme, it reads it, asks you the handful of things only you can answer, you check it, and the dates land in your planner. Hand something to a grown-up in the house and the reminder goes to their phone, not yours.

If you'd like to know when it opens, mail hallo@hartsrus.co.za and we'll put you on the list. No newsletter, no noise — one message when there's something real to see.
